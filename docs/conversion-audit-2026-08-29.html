Finding group A

/pricing

A1
Revenue blocking

The three paid buttons ship disabled

The server HTML for all three plan CTAs contains disabled="" and disabled:opacity-60. They stay inert and visibly faded until React hydrates and /api/me returns — that request measured 0.51–0.57s from this machine, on top of hydration. Anyone who taps in that window gets nothing back.

They are also <button> elements, not links. If hydration never completes — a blocked script, a slow phone, a background tab — they never work at all. The Free card's CTA is a real <Link> and always works, so the only dead controls on the page are the three that take money.

app/pricing/PricingCta.tsx:83 · app/pricing/PricingCta.tsx:87

FixRender each paid CTA as <a href={paymentLink}> so it works as plain HTML, and let the click handler upgrade it — appending client_reference_id, redirecting to login, firing begin_checkout. Keep the spinner for the busy state; drop the initial disabled state entirely.

A2
Revenue blocking

No price is visible for two and a half screens

On a 375 × 812 phone the hero, the four-row free-tier ladder and the watermark comparison consume everything above 2,105px. The watermark demo alone is 1,430px — 1.76 screens of two stacked receipt images. The full page is 6,567px.

Distance from top of document, 375 × 812 viewport. One screen = 812px.

FixPut the price table directly under the hero on mobile and move the watermark comparison below it — the comparison answers “is it worth it?”, which is a question people ask after they've seen the number, not before.

A3
Friction

The watermark demo doesn't do what its own comment says

The demo receipt is narrowed to widthPx: 260 with the comment “Narrowed so both panels sit side by side on a phone.” But the container is grid gap-8 sm:grid-cols-2 — a single column below 640px. Measured on the live page, the two figures sit at y=742 and y=1399: stacked. Mobile pays the cost of the narrowing (a smaller, less legible watermark) and gets none of the benefit.

app/pricing/page.tsx:76 · app/pricing/page.tsx:189

FixMake it grid-cols-2 from the base breakpoint. That is what the 260px width was chosen for, and it also removes ~700px from the scroll depth in A2.

A4
Friction

Four tiers squeezed into 222px columns

At 1440px the grid is lg:grid-cols-4 inside max-w-5xl, so each card measures 222px wide. With p-8 padding that leaves 158px for content, and bullets like “Unlimited AI receipt generation” wrap to three lines. Because the feature lists differ in length, the CTAs don't align — their tops land at 1,684 / 1,676 / 1,621.

FixTwo cards — Free and Pro — with a week / month / year selector inside the Pro card. That gives each card real width, aligns the CTAs by construction, and makes the interval a choice rather than three competing products.

A5
Friction

The $3 anchor is working against the $7.99 plan

Weekly at $3 is the cheapest number on the page and the worst value per month — $13/mo equivalent. It sits left of monthly, so the eye reads $3 → $7.99 → $39 as a rising ladder rather than a falling per-month price. Meanwhile the only badge on the page (“Best value · Save ~60%”) is on the yearly plan, which is a hard sell for a job that is usually one-off: someone who lost a receipt needs it this week, not this year.

FixState every plan's monthly equivalent under its price ($13/mo · $7.99/mo · $3.25/mo) so the ladder reads in one direction. Then check begin_checkout by plan before deciding which card earns the badge — the data to answer this is already being collected.

A6
Friction

The scariest sentence on the page has nothing standing next to it

The cancellation FAQ says there is no self-serve billing portal and that cancelling or refunding means emailing support. That is honest and it should stay. But it is the only thing on the page addressing risk — there is no refund promise, no customer quote, no count of receipts made. The page asks for money and offers nothing to trust.

FixTwo tasks already on the Marketing & Revenue board land exactly here: “Put three real customer quotes on the site” (P1, Next) and “Publish a refund policy” (P2, Next). Put both above the FAQ rather than elsewhere on the site — this is the page where they change a decision.

A7
Friction

The chat bubble covers a quarter of the Decline button

The consent dialog is fixed inset-x-4 bottom-4 z-50 and the chat launcher is fixed bottom-5 right-5 z-50, 56 × 56. On a 375px viewport the Decline button spans x 193–339 and the launcher spans x 303–359: roughly 36px of a 146px button, its full height, sits under the bubble. Same z-index, so paint order decides — and the launcher wins. Confirmed visually on the live page.

components/analytics/ConsentGate.tsx:89 · components/chat/ChatWidget.tsx:139

FixHide the chat launcher while consent is unset, or lift the launcher above the dialog's height. This affects only first-time visitors — which is every visitor, once.

A8
Polish

Screen readers announce “check mark” before every feature

The plan feature lists use a bare <span>✓</span> with no aria-hidden, so it is read aloud on all four cards. NewAccountBanner.tsx already marks its own ✓ as aria-hidden — so two files on the same page disagree.

app/pricing/page.tsx:220, 244, 267, 293

Finding group B

/login and sign-up

The form itself is the best-built thing reviewed here, and none of the findings below are about its markup. They are about what surrounds it: where it sends people, and what it fails to record.

B1
Blind spot

Nothing is recorded when a sign-up fails

There is a whole error-mapping function in LoginForm.tsx, written because real sign-ups were failing with an empty server body and showing a blank red box. Every one of those failures — already-registered, rejected address, rate limit, empty {} — is invisible in analytics. A person hits the wall, leaves, and the funnel shows only a missing sign-up.

app/login/LoginForm.tsx:48–73

FixOne line: track("sign_up_error", { reason }) in the branch that already classifies the error. This is the highest-leverage analytics change on the page, and it pairs directly with the board's “See which checkouts actually turn into sales” (P1, Next).

B2
Blind spot

Google sign-ups never fire sign_up

analytics.signUp("password") is called only on the password path. Google accounts and email-confirmed accounts are both created server-side in the auth callback, which fires nothing. So an entire acquisition channel produces accounts that never appear in the sign-up funnel.

app/login/LoginForm.tsx:271 · app/auth/callback/route.ts:53–78

B3
Blind spot

The login event counts Google at intent and password at success

analytics.signIn("google") fires before signInWithOAuth redirects, so it counts everyone who clicks the button — including those who abandon at Google's account chooser or bounce back with an error. analytics.signIn("password") fires only after a confirmed session. The two are not comparable and Google is systematically inflated.

app/login/LoginForm.tsx:304 vs. app/login/LoginForm.tsx:329

FixMove the Google success event into the auth callback destination, and if the click is worth counting, count it as a separate login_attempt.

B4
Friction

Signing up from the header throws away where you were

The header's log-in link carries the current path — /login?next=/brands/starbucks — but no signup=1. Toggle to “New here? Create an account”, submit, and the redirect branches on the signup prop, which is false, so the new account lands on /pricing?new=1 and next is discarded. Logging in keeps your place; signing up loses it. Someone reading a Starbucks receipt guide is sent to a price list instead of back to the guide.

app/login/LoginForm.tsx:281 · components/layout/Header.tsx:30–34

FixHonour an explicit next whenever one was supplied and isn't the /create default, regardless of which form the person ended up submitting.

B5
Friction

The one-tap path is fourth on the screen

“Continue with Google” sits below the email field, the password field, the primary submit and the mode toggle. It is the fastest way to an account and it reads as the fallback.

FixMove Google above the email field with the “or” divider beneath it. Everything else on the screen stays exactly as it is.

B6
Already right

The form itself passes every check

  • Validation on blur, not on keystroke — and the email check names the actual typo (“Remove the extra dot”)
  • Correct autocomplete per mode: new-password when signing up, current-password when logging in
  • Password reveal toggle at a true 44 × 44 tap target with aria-pressed
  • Visible labels, inline errors wired through aria-describedby, role="alert" on each
  • Focus moves to the verification panel when it replaces the form
  • text-base on mobile, so iOS doesn't zoom on focus
  • Rendered from server props rather than a Suspense boundary — the form is in the initial HTML

Reading the data

Where conversion is actually won

/pricing took 8 impressions and 0 clicks over 90 days. It is not a landing page and there is no point treating it as one — every visitor arrives from inside the product: the header, the watermark modal, the builder's download note, the post-sign-up banner, or the homepage. Its search performance is irrelevant; its first screen is not.

/login returned no rows at all, which is the correct outcome for a funnel step. The noindex treatment of its query-string variants is doing its job and needs nothing further.

/create — the top of the paid funnel — has 1,494 impressions and 0 clicks at an average position of 78. That is a ranking problem, not a page problem, and it already sits in memory as such. It matters here only because it means the paid funnel is currently fed almost entirely by blog and brand pages, so anything that breaks between “I made a receipt” and “I paid” costs the whole month's content work.

Downstream instrumentation is genuinely good: download_click → watermark_prompt → upgrade_click → begin_checkout → purchase_landed → pro_activated covers the money path end to end, including manual fulfilment. The blind spot is the auth step sitting in the middle of it — which is what B1 through B3 are about.

Order of work

What to fix first

  1. Make the paid CTAs work as plain HTMLA1. Nothing else on this list matters if the button doesn't respond. Smallest change, largest downside removed.
  2. Record sign-up failures and fix the Google eventsB1–B3. Until these land, every other change on this page is being judged on numbers that don't include the people it's failing.
  3. Put the price above the watermark comparison on mobileA2 and A3 together. Fixing the stacked grid alone removes about 700px; reordering removes the rest.
  4. Keep next through sign-upB4. A one-line condition that stops sending mid-journey readers to a price list.
  5. Then the trust work already on the boardA6. Quotes and a refund policy, placed on /pricing rather than elsewhere. Both are already written up as tasks.

Steps 1, 2 and 4 are each small enough to ship together in one pass. Step 3 is a layout change worth reviewing on a real phone before it merges to main.
